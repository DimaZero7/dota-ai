## Two practical experiments

**After a significant successful action, name the next available team result and what it requires.** Select three moments for review, including one where leaving or farming was reasonable. Record the plan, whether it was achievable with the team state, the actual next action, and the result. Success means a justified decision, not mandatory tower damage or a win.

The baseline contains contrasting outcomes of activity; the number of genuinely missed opportunities has not been measured. Three moments are an exercise design, not a statistical norm. Progress remains untested.

**Before returning after a death or failed engagement, name what has changed:** nearby allies, known threats, the objective, or an available resource area. If nothing changed, examine another task first. Review one unsuccessful and one successful return, testing the available alternative and the cost of the decision.

Candidate cases are two difficult Jakiro games and late DP; positive comparisons include Jakiro against Io and NS. The frequency of erroneous returns is unknown. Fewer deaths without useful participation would not demonstrate improvement. Progress remains untested.

## What the results can hide

Nine wins can hide expensive passages inside successful games. A useful next skill is recognizing the difference between substantial personal activity and the team obtaining the result it needs. Winning does not validate every entry, and losing does not invalidate every entry.

Restored involvement after a difficult opening is an observed gameplay feature. It does not justify a psychological label. This is a working interpretation of a recent sample; decision constraints, visibility, available alternatives and mechanical execution remain incompletely examined.
